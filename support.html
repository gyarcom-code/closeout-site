Setting up your job

Settings → Jobs → Add a Job. What matters:

  • Hourly rate — what your employer actually pays you per hour before tips, not the minimum wage.
  • State — decides which minimum wage applies and whether a tip credit is allowed where you work.
  • Pay week starts — the day your employer's workweek begins. The minimum-wage check is done per pay week, so this has to be right.
  • Starter preset — the closest schedule to your venue. It is a starting point, not a commitment.

My tip-out is not one of the presets

Settings → Jobs → Edit Job → Tip-Out Rules. Add, edit, reorder and remove rules freely. Each rule can be a percentage of a base, a fixed amount, or a share of a points pool.

Percentages take two decimal places, so 1.22% and 0.98% work exactly as written. Rules are independent: each is calculated against your original shift figures, never against what is left after another rule.

If a points rule will not save, set your own points on the job screen first. A points pool divides by the total points including yours — with yours at zero, the rule would hand over the entire amount.

Why is the app asking me for my local minimum wage?

Because in your state, cities set their own, and the state figure would be too low. Rather than quietly measuring you against a number that does not apply where you work, the app says it does not know and asks.

Until you answer, the wage check reports that it cannot determine the answer — not that everything is fine. Those are different things, and the app will never confuse them.

What does "your employer may owe you" mean?

In most states an employer may pay a tipped worker below the ordinary minimum wage, on the condition that tips make up the difference — measured over each workweek, not each shift. When they do not, the employer owes the shortfall.

CloseOut compares your week's hours against the applicable minimum wage, and your actual earnings — wages, service charges and the tips you kept after tip-out — against that threshold. If there is a gap, it says so and shows what the figure was built from.

It is a calculation, not legal advice, and the app is not a lawyer. What you do with the number is yours to decide.

Getting your data out

Settings → Your data → Export CSV. Free, always, with no purchase and no limit. Every recorded figure and every tip-out line is in the file.

The app also writes a plain CSV backup to your own iCloud Drive once a week, so your records outlive the app whether or not you ever open this menu.

Syncing between devices

Sync happens through your own iCloud account, with no sign-up and nothing to configure. Your devices must be signed into the same Apple ID.

One thing worth knowing: if you edit the same shift on two devices before they have synced, the later edit wins and the earlier device's changes to that record are discarded — all of them, not merged field by field. This is how Apple's sync works and the app cannot override it.

Known limits

  • United States only. The wage and tax rules are US federal and state law and mean nothing elsewhere.
  • Tax figures are estimates. They are there to help you set money aside and check your own return, not to file it.
  • City minimum wages are not shipped. There are hundreds and they change independently. The app knows which states have them and asks you rather than guessing.
